# Tailor Studio — photo map

The site is designed to accept a mixed set of real phone photographs, licensed editorial images and a small number of selective AI atmosphere images. Phone photographs do not need to be edited before delivery.

## Selection rule

- About 80% real photography and no more than 20% selective AI atmosphere.
- Prefer people in motion, daily life, transport, dining and venues in use.
- Keep architectural and landscape images as breathing spaces.
- Do not present stock or AI people as Tailor Studio clients.
- Minimum useful size: 1600 px on the long edge. Original phone files are preferred.

## Primary slots

| Slot | Preferred subject | Crop and safe area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | A living Beijing or Shanghai scene with people or visible movement | Wide desktop and tall mobile crop; keep the right edge visually calm because the headline crosses it |
| Street life | Candid city scene, pedestrians, bicycles, market or neighbourhood | Portrait 4:5; one clear visual subject |
| Human detail | Food, hands, tea, material or small cultural detail | Portrait or square; close framing |
| Format preview | Real working, dining or shared travel moment | Portrait 4:5; avoid staged corporate stock |
| Beijing | Heritage meeting contemporary city life | Flexible 4:5 or square; centre-safe composition |
| Shanghai | Design, dining, skyline or active street life | Flexible 4:5 or square; centre-safe composition |
| Greater Bay Area | Modern architecture, transport, industry or technology | Flexible 4:5 or square; centre-safe composition |
| Chengdu | Tea culture, food, greenery or relaxed public life | Flexible 4:5 or square; centre-safe composition |
| Sourcing | Hotel, venue, restaurant or transport with people present | Square or landscape; avoid empty showroom imagery |
| Approach | Authentic travel moment that feels observed rather than staged | Wide landscape; visual detail may sit off-centre |
| Menu | Strong portrait image representing contemporary China | Tall portrait with simple edges for navigation text |

## When the photographs arrive

1. Preserve every original file.
2. Make a contact sheet and shortlist before editing.
3. Assign images by subject and crop rather than by filename.
4. Correct exposure and colour gently; keep natural differences between cameras and locations.
5. Rebalance copy only after the final image sequence is visible.
